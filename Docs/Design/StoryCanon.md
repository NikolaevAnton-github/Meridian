# Story foundation

Owner-supplied narrative recorded on 2026-09-13. Preserve the distinction
between fixed facts, working connective proposals and unresolved details.
"Parautilitarians" is the English working term for people with these abilities.
The source draft used ProjectX; these notes belong to the current
MeridianSquad repository and do not establish a final game title.

## World: fixed facts

The setting is the near future. People with extraordinary abilities have
appeared. They are classified by ability strength and danger; they are
eliminated or held in a special prison on a remote ocean island.

MERIDIAN is a secret government unit of specially trained parautilitarians.
The protagonist is a member. Exactly four people occupy the highest danger
category. This is a headcount, not four classes or a promised set of bosses.
One of the four can reshape surrounding space through thought. His escape
and subsequent attack initiate the story.

## Fixed sequence

1. The spatial parautilitarian escapes the island prison and hides on neutral
   territory beyond the reach of the pursuing US and UN structures. This is a
   premise of the fictional world; the territory and its government are unnamed.
2. He prepares revenge and gathers a loyal private military company (PMC) army.
   Using spatial distortion, he arrives with his soldiers in central New York.
3. He deliberately launches an attack and causes a catastrophe. During the
   confrontation he suffers a severe head wound. Who wounds him and how remain
   unspecified.
4. His soldiers retreat with him into a skyscraper and carry him to its top.
5. After that ascent, a discharge isolates the building from the outside world.
   He remains alive but no longer controls the process. Spatial distortion
   grows stronger closer to him. This is loss of control following the wound,
   not a deliberate series of trials arranged for the protagonist.
6. MERIDIAN is sent to capture or eliminate him. The unit arrives as the area
   catastrophically closes. The squad dies; the protagonist survives and is
   trapped on the ground/first floor. The precise staging remains unresolved.
7. The protagonist has no contact with command and finds no accessible way out.
   He obtains information locally, including surveillance recordings.
8. Footage shows soldiers carrying the wounded target upstairs, followed by
   the discharge. The protagonist connects the target to the catastrophe and
   establishes a direction for his search.
9. With no other available course, he independently chooses to continue the
   original mission. He does not know whether the target's death will stop
   the distortion or open an exit. He receives no new order or confirmation.
10. He ascends through increasingly distorted spaces toward the source. PMC
    soldiers protect their wounded leader and prevent the protagonist from
    reaching and killing him.

The original wording remains "capture or eliminate." Continuing the operation
does not create an order to kill at any cost. It also does not commission a
capture mechanic, ending selection, or mandatory moral branch. Whether the
protagonist's decision is expressed through speech, action or UI is undecided.

## Working connective proposals

These proposals were supplied as prior team development in the owner's brief.
They support coherence but are not additional fixed user-authored facts.
Specific biographies, names, dialogue and scene staging remain to be developed.

| Connection | Working proposal |
| --- | --- |
| Refuge before the attack | A closed fictional enclave protected by a local regime that refuses extradition; no real country is assigned. |
| PMC loyalty | A contractor abandoned the core unit to die. The parautilitarian risked himself to save them; they later built the organization together. Personal debt exceeds an ordinary contract. |
| Defense after the catastrophe | Commander and veterans refuse to abandon their rescuer. They hope he will wake and open an exit. Ordinary soldiers may fear, doubt or disagree. |
| Army transfer | He temporarily connects the staging base to a previously studied entry point in New York; people and vehicles cross the short passage. |
| Carrying him upstairs | The tower's upper platform is a fallback rooftop evacuation point. As his power becomes unreliable, soldiers carry him there; the discharge cuts off evacuation. |
| Active resistance | A PMC commander organizes the defense while a doctor tries to keep the wounded leader alive. Soldiers make tactical decisions themselves. |

For controlled spatial power before the injury, proposed constraints are
knowledge of both connected places and attention to sustain a complex passage.
After the wound, distortion is involuntary. Do not explain every rearrangement
as conscious tactics. Future combat should retain readable local spaces and
signals of dangerous rearrangement; their design is still a proposal.

Do not add mind control, resurrection, time control by the spatial antagonist,
or rewritten history. The player's time slowdown is a separately requested
gameplay ability, not a change to the antagonist's established power.

## Tone, retired explanations and open details

The tone is realistic and dark, with escalating spatial disturbances.
Control and F.E.A.R. guide atmosphere and combat. The Evil Within/STEM and the
owner's wounded-brain analogy guide the image of lost control. Their technology,
characters and universes do not enter this setting. Earlier ideas of a literal
building-body, immortality through the tower, or consciously maintaining a
refuge after isolation are no longer the operative explanation.

Morrow Tower is a working name only. Open details include the building's age,
exact year, the fugitive's name and biography, specific revenge target, prison,
classification scales and agencies, PMC composition, cause of the head wound,
the squad's death and protagonist's survival, and the consequences of the
target's death. Revenge, intentional attack and subsequent loss of control
are already fixed and must not be reopened as alternative premises.
