## Objective and dependencies

After [ED-02](ED-02.md), make the first specimen's debris stable, bounded and
resettable in the lab copy, covering both cladding and concrete from the
[layered column](../../Approvals/EnvironmentDestruction01-ColumnLayers01.json).
Provide one reproducible rubble fixture with explicit
support semantics for later traversal work; this task does not prove new locomotion
or enemy traversal behavior.

## Work and deliverables

- Classify fragments as solid static support, eligible movable support, unstable
  or too-small pieces, and nonblocking cosmetic debris. Record count, mass/impulse,
  collision, sleep and lifetime limits. Retain a representative physics-enabled
  piece that can move; do not make all rubble cosmetic or permanently freeze it.
- Extend ED-02's basic fragment collision to repeated mixed-material falls and
  piles. Large visible pieces must collide with the world and each other and
  retain collision while sleeping; verify that a new falling piece can contact
  and wake resting movable rubble where appropriate. Any disabled/replaced state
  needs an explicit collision/visual policy. Tune collision proxies, contact
  stability, friction, bounce and sleep from evidence, not blanket mutual ignore.
- Define support identity/lifetime and invalidation on further fracture, movement
  where relevant, replacement, cleanup and reset. Consume the applicable MSQ-96
  contact contract if delivered; otherwise document the bounded handoff seam and
  pending integration without starting MSQ-96 or replacing its locomotion scope.
- Implement bounded cleanup and reset for the specimen and its spawned components,
  effects and collision state. Cosmetic cleanup must preserve the visible meaning
  of damage; meaningful support/blocking must not vanish silently under an occupant.
  Record the intended policy and limitation for that transition.
- Save a reproducible fixture configuration and a short route identifying the
  supported patch, movable piece and excluded unstable debris for MSQ-78 and
  applicable MSQ-95/96 consumers. Document how to restore the fixture exactly.

## Acceptance and evidence

- A focused destruction/settling sequence stays within declared fragment and
  physics limits, without explosive motion or a permanent unintended route block.
  Mixed cladding/concrete piles do not retain visible interpenetration, overlap
  flicker or sustained jitter. Include a new fragment falling onto settled rubble
  and normal/slowdown transitions for affected sleep and cleanup behavior.
- A representative movable support responds physically; its support identity is
  invalidated correctly when removed/replaced. Actual traversal remains explicitly
  pending in the owning tasks.
- A declared short repeated destroy/reset sequence shows no sustained growth in
  live actors, components, simulated bodies or memory after settling/reset. Compare
  the first and final cycle and investigate a rising trend rather than hiding it
  behind a fixed cycle count.
- Cleanup and reset do not leave stale hit blockers, support handles or duplicate
  intact specimens. Retest ED-02's hole/cover checks only where affected.
- Store the fixture manifest, support/cleanup contract, count and timing traces,
  focused footage and identity under
  `Saved/EnvironmentDestruction01/ED-03/<Candidate>/`. Identify commit/dirty scope,
  lab map and actors, derived assets/fingerprints, tuning, actual runtime/worker
  settings, capture window and outstanding consumer integration.
