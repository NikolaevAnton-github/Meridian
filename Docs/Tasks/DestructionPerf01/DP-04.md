## Objective

Bound destruction presentation work while preserving readable impact, material and
damage cues. The Niagara-disabled warm run still measured 23.0 FPS during 0-1 s,
38.3 FPS during 1-5 s and 26.20 ms Game time during 1-5 s. Disabling activation
did not resolve the warm CPU bottleneck; asset loading/component setup remained.
Use [baseline evidence](../DestructionPerf01.md#isolation-and-trace-evidence) and
DP-02 attribution to select useful changes, without assuming VFX is the main cause.

## Bounded work

- Build a project-owned presentation policy that groups events by affected area,
  material and a short time window, prioritizing nearby important damage and
  representative energetic or large breaks for sound and dust.
- Derive explicit per-frame and concurrent effect/voice limits from measured load
  sweeps and quality comparisons. Record requested, emitted, merged, dropped and
  active presentation events/instances; do not invent universal numerical quotas.
- Apply measured audio concurrency, distance and energy thresholds. Deduplicate
  optional identical material updates only while retaining correct visible state.
- Use Niagara Effect Types and distance/significance budgets where supported and
  justified by the measured workload. Keep persistent damage cues readable.
- Evaluate component pooling only when reuse/allocation cost warrants it. Cap pool
  size and memory, separate prewarm cost from the measured event, and retain cold
  measurements. A shared Niagara/data-channel candidate requires evidence that
  instance creation/execution cost makes it useful; neither option is mandatory.
- Keep required gameplay callbacks and collision/profile publication in
  [DP-03](DP-03.md). Presentation merging/dropping must not suppress gameplay damage,
  fracture, collision changes or sleep/wake behavior.

## Acceptance and verification

- Compare each adopted policy independently through DP-01/DP-02, recording warm
  first-five-second and phase statistics, VFX instances, voices and allocation
  peaks where available. Report unavailable counters and run-to-run variability.
- Show the same intended affected area, major openings and fracture outcome;
  stochastic fragments need not match exactly. Dust and sound still communicate
  important impacts; weaker blasts or disabled effects are diagnostic controls.
- Demonstrate that presentation bursts and concurrent resources respect the
  measured policy as event load grows, while preserving useful nearby cues.
- Check repeated blasts and reset during active effects/audio for stale callbacks,
  leaked instances/voices, stale material state and incomplete pooled-object reset.
  Verify aggregation/lifetimes under normal time and the common slowdown contract.
- Run focused source/build and runtime checks when execution is authorized; final
  visual/audio quality and gameplay acceptance belong to the owner.
